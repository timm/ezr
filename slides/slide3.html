3. Rare classes: read a third, find nine tenths

About 1% of these papers are a “yes”. Accuracy is useless — say “no” to everything and score 99%. Precision is useless too, and unavoidably so: at that base rate, catching nearly every hit means handing over a lot of misses. The question a reviewer actually asks is how much must I read?

min.py — complement naive bayes


    
Score a class by what it is not like.  With 45 yes
    against 1659 no, the complement of “yes” is estimated from
    plenty of data; the class itself is not.  Features are the 64 words with
    the most tf·idf.

read = the share of the held-out papers the model
    hands over.  vs random = recall divided by that share: screening at
    random, reading 36% finds 36%.  So Hall costs a third of the reading for
    93% of the hits.

    
Spending more than ten labels does not help, and on
    Kitchenham it hurts (85% → 55% at 50 labels).  The learner asks for
    whatever looks most like a yes, so the labelled set fills with yeses and
    the “no” side — the side complement NB actually
    estimates — starves.
